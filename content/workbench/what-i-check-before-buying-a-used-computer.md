A used computer can be a great deal or somebody else’s problem with a price on it. Most of the difference shows up in a few minutes of checking before any money changes hands.

## Topics in this note

- Does it power on and boot without errors?
- Storage health and how much life the drive has left
- Battery condition on laptops
- Screen, keyboard, ports, hinges, and fans
- Signs of liquid damage or opened cases
- Whether it’s locked to a previous owner’s account
- If it can run the operating system you actually want

None of this guarantees a good machine, but it catches most of the expensive surprises.

## What I learned

Account locks and worn-out drives cause more trouble than slow processors. Check those first.

## What I’d do differently

I’d bring a USB drive with a test system on it, so I can check the hardware even if the installed system is a mess.

## Tools & Parts (examples)

- USB drive with a bootable test system
- Phone charger or USB device to test ports
- Flashlight for checking inside ports and vents
- A short written checklist
