Plenty of equipment gets replaced while it still works, or after a small, fixable failure. Cleaning it up, testing it, and putting it back to work is satisfying, and it usually teaches me something about how it was built.

## Topics in this note

- Cleaning before judging: dust, grime, and dirty controls
- Testing safely before trusting a device again
- Small fixes that bring things back: fuses, switches, connectors
- Finding a new job for older gear
- Recycling what really can’t be saved

Reused doesn’t have to mean worse. A lot of older gear was built to be opened and serviced.

## What I learned

A careful clean and a basic test bring back more equipment than I expected.

## What I’d do differently

I’d keep notes on what each piece needed, so I know what to check first on similar gear.

## Tools & Parts (examples)

- Brushes and compressed air
- Contact cleaner
- Multimeter
- Replacement fuses and switches
